## Apps & tools, by the leak they actually fix

### Gas — you already track this in the app
**GasBuddy** (free, iOS/Android) — crowdsourced live gas prices at every station
near you, sorts cheapest-first. Pair it with the ⛽ gas log already in
FLOWSTATE's Car card: check GasBuddy before you fill up, log the real fill in
the app. A few weeks of that turns your $250 gas budget from a guess into a
number you can trust.

### Groceries — you shop at RCSS (Real Canadian Superstore = Loblaws)
- **PC Optimum** — you're already a Loblaws customer (RCSS #1091 shows up
  repeatedly in your statements) and probably not using the loyalty program.
  Free to join, stacks with sales, points redeem as instant discounts. This is
  the single easiest win on this list — you're leaving money on a table you
  already shop at.
- **Flipp** (free) — aggregates every store's weekly flyer in one app so you
  can check what's actually on sale before the Saturday grocery run, instead of
  buying at list price.
- **Checkout 51 / Caddle** — scan grocery receipts, get small cashback on
  select items. Not huge (a few dollars a trip) but it's free money for a scan
  you'd do anyway.

### Dining / "I want food but don't want to cook"
- **Too Good To Go** (free) — restaurants and grocery stores near London, ON
  sell surplus food (bakery, prepared meals, groceries near best-before) at
  40–70% off. If you're going to spend money on food out, this is a genuinely
  cheaper alternative to full-price Uber Eats for the nights willpower runs low
  — not a replacement for cooking, a harm-reduction option for when cooking
  isn't happening anyway.
- The existing [GROCERY-LIST.md](GROCERY-LIST.md) meal plan in this repo is
  still the actual fix for the $155→$63 grocery number that's dropped too low —
  the delivery money didn't become groceries, it became cheaper takeout. That
  needs to flip.

### Banking — get rid of fees you're paying for nothing
- **EQ Bank** or **Simplii Financial** — genuinely no monthly fee, no minimum
  balance, unlimited transactions, and EQ pays actual interest on the balance
  sitting in chequing. Either is a real alternative to the $16.95/mo CIBC plan
  if the fee-waiver minimum doesn't work for your cash flow.
- **KOHO** — prepaid Visa + spending account with instant cashback (0.5–2% on
  purchases) and automatic round-up savings (every purchase rounds up to the
  next dollar, the difference goes into a savings pot automatically). Good fit
  for you specifically because it turns "I already spent the money" into small
  automatic savings without you having to think about it.

### Subscriptions — the leak that creeps
- **Rocket Money** (or just a recurring calendar reminder) — surfaces every
  recurring charge on a linked card so nothing hides for three months like WFG
  and DashPass did. If it doesn't fully support CIBC well, a manual quarterly
  "read the last statement top to bottom" habit does the same job — you clearly
  can spot these once you look (you found PixivFanbox and Apollo yourself).

### Online shopping (Amazon, etc.)
- **Rakuten** (free browser extension/app) — automatic cashback (typically
  1–10%) on purchases at thousands of retailers including Amazon, activated
  before you check out. Set-and-forget.

### Insurance shopping
- **Ratehub.ca**, **Kanetix.ca**, **LowestRates.ca** — free comparison quotes
  for auto insurance. $191/mo is on the higher side for a used Civic and a
  15-minute comparison could realistically save $30–50/mo. Note: Ontario's auto
  insurance rules changed July 1, 2026 (accident-benefits changes) — a
  lowestrates.ca email about it is already sitting in your inbox, worth a skim
  before you compare quotes so you know what changed.
